I am a Computer Science Ph.D. candidate at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, advised by Prof. [Elahe Soltanaghai](https://elahe.web.illinois.edu/#) in the Wireless, Sensing, and Embedded Networked Systems Lab. My research builds differentiable, physics-grounded models of how wireless signals propagate through real environments, replacing expensive electromagnetic simulation with fast, learned surrogates that stay tied to the underlying physics.

My work spans two settings. **WiNeRF** (accepted to EWSN 2026) learns a continuous radiance-field representation of indoor wireless propagation from sparse channel measurements, enabling accurate multipath prediction without dense site surveys. My current project extends this idea outdoors, developing a learned, physically-grounded model of how radio waves scatter off vegetation, so outdoor wireless simulators can handle real environments without simulating leaf-by-leaf geometry. Both are instances of the same idea: replace an intractable explicit simulation with a compact, differentiable surrogate that stays grounded in physics.

I'm also part of **WiViz**, which studies how interactive visualizations can make wireless mechanisms legible to non-experts.

I completed my BS in Electrical Engineering with high distinction from the Lahore University of Management Sciences (LUMS) in 2022. Prior to my Ph.D., I gained over a year of experience as a Research Assistant in the Smart Data Systems and Applications Laboratory at LUMS, where I worked on projects related to wireless sensing and machine learning.
